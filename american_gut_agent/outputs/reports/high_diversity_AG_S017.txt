# MICROBIOME HEALTH REPORT

## Sample Summary

This report analyzes sample AG_S017, collected from a 67-year-old female omnivore with a normal BMI.

## Community Composition

The microbiome of sample AG_S017 is dominated by several key genera:
*   **Roseburia** (4.97% relative abundance): A beneficial bacterium known for producing butyrate, a short-chain fatty acid crucial for gut health.
*   **Eubacterium** (4.87% relative abundance): Another butyrate-producing bacterium, often associated with a healthy gut.
*   **Faecalibacterium** (3.64% relative abundance): Known for its anti-inflammatory properties and butyrate production, generally considered a marker of gut health.
*   **Akkermansia** (3.63% relative abundance): A mucin-degrading bacterium that plays a role in maintaining the gut barrier and is often associated with leanness and improved metabolic health.
*   **Clostridium** (3.47% relative abundance): While some species are pathogenic, many are beneficial butyrate producers.
*   **Prevotella** (3.35% relative abundance): Often associated with plant-rich diets and carbohydrate fermentation.

## Diversity Assessment vs Cohort Reference Ranges

The sample exhibits healthy alpha-diversity:
*   **Shannon diversity**: 4.15 (Cohort range: 3.62 - 4.23). This indicates a rich and even microbial community.
*   **Observed OTUs**: 110 (Cohort range: 105.46 - 111.87). This is within the healthy range, suggesting a good number of distinct microbial species.
*   **Chao1 richness**: 110 (Cohort range: 102.40 - 120.22). This metric, estimating total species richness, falls within the expected range.
*   **Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio**: 1.92 (Cohort range: -14.58 - 27.58). This ratio is well within the typical range, indicating a balanced community structure.

The overall diversity assessment indicates that the microbiome is **not dysbiotic**.

## Top 3 Flagged Taxa

The following genera were present in unusually high relative abundance compared to the cohort average:

*   **Roseburia (0.0497 vs cohort mean 0.0098)**: This significant increase in Roseburia is generally positive, as Roseburia species are known butyrate producers. Butyrate is a primary energy source for colonocytes and has anti-inflammatory effects. High levels are often associated with a healthy gut.
*   **Eubacterium (0.0487 vs cohort mean 0.0114)**: Similar to Roseburia, Eubacterium species are important for producing beneficial short-chain fatty acids like butyrate. An elevated level suggests a robust population of these beneficial bacteria.
*   **Akkermansia (0.0363 vs cohort mean 0.0047)**: A significantly higher abundance of Akkermansia muciniphila is often considered beneficial. This bacterium strengthens the gut barrier by stimulating mucin production and is associated with improved metabolic health and weight management.

## Dietary Recommendations

Based on the prominent and beneficial taxa, here are some dietary recommendations to support your microbiome:

*   **Increase Dietary Fiber**: To further support butyrate-producing bacteria like Roseburia and Eubacterium, incorporate a variety of high-fiber foods such as fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes. (PMID: 24336217, 2014)
*   **Embrace Polyphenol-Rich Foods**: Foods rich in polyphenols, such as berries, dark chocolate, green tea, and red wine, can selectively promote the growth of beneficial bacteria like Akkermansia and Roseburia.
*   **Consider a Mediterranean-style Diet**: This diet, rich in plant-based foods, olive oil, and fiber, has been shown to enhance the growth of beneficial species including Eubacterium and Faecalibacterium. (PMID: 37432307, 2023)
*   **Avoid Ultra-Processed Foods**: These foods, often high in synthetic additives and low in fiber, have been linked to decreased microbial diversity and lower levels of beneficial bacteria like Akkermansia. (PMID: 40077728, 2025)

## Probiotic Suggestions

Given the strong presence of beneficial genera, targeting specific probiotics may further enhance gut health:

*   **Probiotics containing *Bifidobacterium* and *Lactobacillus* species**: While not flagged high, these are well-researched probiotics. Some *Lactobacillus plantarum* strains have shown to positively modulate gut microbiota composition, including increasing beneficial bacteria, which could indirectly support the existing healthy community. (PMID: 37033942, 2023)
*   **Future Probiotics like *Akkermansia* or *Roseburia***: Research suggests that *Roseburia* and *Akkermansia* show promise as future probiotics due to their significant health benefits. While not widely available as standalone probiotics yet, their high abundance in your sample is a positive indicator. (PMID: 31296969, 2019)

## Overall Microbiome Health Score: 9/10

This microbiome is in excellent health, characterized by high diversity, no dysbiosis, and a high abundance of key beneficial, butyrate-producing bacteria like Roseburia and Eubacterium, as well as the gut-barrier strengthening Akkermansia.
