## Sample Summary
Sample AG_S017 belongs to a 67-year-old female with a normal BMI, who identifies as an omnivore. The sample was collected from feces.

## Community Composition
The dominant genera in this sample include `Roseburia` (4.97%), `Eubacterium` (4.87%), `Faecalibacterium` (3.64%), `Akkermansia` (3.63%), and `Clostridium` (3.47%). Many of these, like `Roseburia` and `Faecalibacterium`, are known for producing beneficial short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like butyrate, which are crucial for gut health and energy for colon cells. `Akkermansia` is a mucin-degrading bacterium often associated with a healthy gut barrier and metabolic health.

## Diversity Assessment vs Cohort Reference Ranges
The sample exhibits healthy alpha-diversity metrics, all falling within the established cohort reference ranges.
- **Shannon diversity:** 4.15 (Cohort range: 3.62 - 4.23)
- **Observed OTUs:** 110 (Cohort range: 105.46 - 111.87)
- **Chao1 index:** 110 (Cohort range: 102.40 - 120.22)
- **Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio:** 1.92 (Cohort range: -14.58 - 27.58)

The sample is not flagged as dysbiotic, indicating a balanced microbial community structure.

## Top 3 Flagged Taxa
Several taxa in this sample show relative abundances notably higher than the cohort mean, which can sometimes indicate a beneficial profile.
1.  **Roseburia** (Relative Abundance: 4.97%, Delta from Cohort Mean: +3.99%) - `Roseburia` species are known butyrate producers, playing a significant role in maintaining gut barrier integrity and anti-inflammatory processes. High levels are generally considered beneficial for gut health.
2.  **Eubacterium** (Relative Abundance: 4.87%, Delta from Cohort Mean: +3.73%) - Certain `Eubacterium` species are also important butyrate producers and contribute to a healthy gut environment.
3.  **Akkermansia** (Relative Abundance: 3.63%, Delta from Cohort Mean: +3.16%) - `Akkermansia muciniphila` is a beneficial bacterium that resides in the gut mucus layer. Its abundance is often inversely correlated with obesity and metabolic disorders, and it's associated with a healthy gut lining.

## Dietary Recommendations
Based on the prominent and flagged taxa, particularly the beneficial butyrate producers (`Roseburia`, `Eubacterium`) and `Akkermansia`, increasing intake of specific dietary components can further support their growth and activity:

*   **Increase dietary fiber, especially resistant starches and prebiotics:** Foods rich in resistant starch (e.g., green bananas, cooled potatoes/rice) and other fibers (e.g., oats, legumes) provide fuel for butyrate-producing bacteria like `Roseburia` and `Eubacterium`. (PMID: 24336217, 2014)
*   **Consume polyphenols:** Found in berries, grapes, dark chocolate, and green tea, polyphenols have been shown to positively influence gut microbiota, potentially supporting beneficial microbes. (PMID: 31296969, 2019)
*   **Embrace a plant-rich diet:** A diet high in plant products, similar to the Mediterranean diet, can promote the growth of beneficial Firmicutes that metabolize plant polysaccharides, including `Roseburia` and `Eubacterium`. (PMID: 24336217, 2014; PMID: 37432307, 2023)
*   **Limit ultra-processed foods (UPFs):** UPFs are associated with decreased microbial diversity and lower levels of beneficial bacteria like `Akkermansia`. Reducing their intake can help maintain a healthy gut. (PMID: 40077728, 2025)

## Probiotic Suggestions
While your microbiome appears healthy, certain probiotics might offer additional targeted benefits:

*   **Probiotics containing *Lactobacillus plantarum*:** Specific strains like *Lactobacillus plantarum* JYLP-326 have shown potential in modulating gut microbiota and improving mental states. (PMID: 37033942, 2023)
*   **Probiotics with *Bifidobacterium* species:** *Bifidobacterium* species are well-known beneficial probiotics. Studies suggest that certain *Bifidobacterium* combinations, such as *Bifidobacterium bifidum* BGN4 and *Bifidobacterium longum* BORI, can improve cognitive function and mood in older adults while reducing inflammation-causing bacteria. (PMID: 32300799, 2021)
*   **Next-generation probiotics like *Akkermansia*:** `Akkermansia` itself is considered a promising future probiotic due to its strong links with gut health and metabolic benefits. (PMID: 31296969, 2019)

## Overall Microbiome Health Score: 9/10
This sample's microbiome health score is excellent due to high diversity across all measured metrics, absence of a dysbiotic flag, and a strong presence of beneficial, short-chain fatty acid-producing bacteria like `Roseburia` and `Eubacterium`, as well as `Akkermansia`.
