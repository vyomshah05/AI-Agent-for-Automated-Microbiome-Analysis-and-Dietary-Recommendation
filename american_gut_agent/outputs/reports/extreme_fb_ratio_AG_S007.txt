# MICROBIOME HEALTH REPORT

## Sample Summary
This report analyzes sample ID AG_S007, from a 54-year-old female following a vegan diet, categorized as underweight. The sample was collected from feces.

## Community Composition
The dominant genera in this sample's microbiome include *Streptococcus*, *Ruminococcus*, *Faecalibacterium*, *Eubacterium*, *Clostridium*, *Blautia*, and *Lactobacillus*. Notably, a significant proportion of the community belongs to the Firmicutes phylum. While some of these, like *Faecalibacterium*, are known for producing beneficial short-chain fatty acids, the overall dominance of Firmicutes and specific genera like *Ruminococcus* and *Clostridium* can be indicative of an imbalanced gut environment.

## Diversity Assessment vs Cohort Reference Ranges
The alpha-diversity metrics for sample AG_S007 show:
*   **Shannon diversity**: 3.66, which is at the lower end of the cohort reference range (3.62 - 4.23).
*   **Observed OTUs**: 109, falling within the cohort reference range (105.46 - 111.87).
*   **Chao1 index**: 109, also within the cohort reference range (102.40 - 120.22).

However, a significant finding is the **Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio of 65.86**, which is considerably higher than the cohort's high reference range of 27.58. This elevated ratio leads to a classification of the sample as **dysbiotic**, indicating an imbalance in the microbial community.

## Top 3 Flagged Taxa
Several genera were found at unusually high relative abundances compared to the cohort mean. The most prominent include:

*   ***Ruminococcus***: With a relative abundance of 0.064 in this sample compared to a cohort mean of 0.0096. High levels of *Ruminococcus* species have been associated with pro-inflammatory responses and are often linked to diets high in processed foods and animal-derived products (PMID: 33811041, 2021; PMID: 31315227, 2019).
*   ***Eubacterium***: This genus shows a relative abundance of 0.058 in the sample, significantly higher than the cohort mean of 0.0089. While some *Eubacterium* species, such as *Eubacterium rectale*, are beneficial butyrate producers and are supported by plant-rich diets (PMID: 24336217, 2014; PMID: 37432307, 2023), their unusually high overall abundance in a dysbiotic context warrants attention.
*   ***Clostridium***: The sample shows a *Clostridium* relative abundance of 0.055, markedly above the cohort mean of 0.0097. High abundances of *Clostridium* are associated with dysbiosis, often linked to high-fat/carbohydrate diets, contributing to metabolic inflammation (PMID: 32008579, 2020).

## Dietary Recommendations
Given the observed dysbiosis, particularly the high Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio and elevated levels of *Ruminococcus* and *Clostridium*, the following dietary adjustments may be beneficial:

*   **Increase diverse dietary fiber intake**: Emphasize a wide variety of whole, unprocessed plant foods to foster a balanced and diverse gut microbiome. Diets rich in plant polysaccharides can support beneficial Firmicutes species while potentially mitigating the impact of less desirable ones (PMID: 24336217, 2014).
*   **Focus on whole, unprocessed plant foods**: Even within a vegan diet, prioritize whole foods over processed vegan alternatives. This can help reduce pro-inflammatory *Ruminococcus* and *Clostridium* species often linked to processed and animal-derived food patterns (PMID: 33811041, 2021; PMID: 32008579, 2020).
*   **Adopt a Mediterranean dietary pattern**: While maintaining a vegan approach, incorporate the principles of the Mediterranean diet which emphasizes healthy fats, diverse plant foods, and whole grains, and has been shown to reduce Firmicutes and enhance beneficial short-chain fatty acid producers (PMID: 37432307, 2023).
*   **Monitor fat and carbohydrate quality**: Ensure that fats and carbohydrates come from healthy, unrefined sources. Diets high in unhealthy fats and refined carbohydrates can contribute to *Clostridium* predominance and dysbiosis (PMID: 32008579, 2020).

## Probiotic Suggestions
Consider incorporating probiotic strains that are shown to counteract the observed imbalances:

*   **Bifidobacterium-containing probiotics**: Probiotics with *Bifidobacterium* have been linked to reductions in inflammation-causing gut bacteria and improvements in cognitive function in older adults, and have shown a negative correlation with *Eubacterium* levels (PMID: 32300799, 2021). They can also help rebalance microbiomes dominated by *Clostridium* (PMID: 32008579, 2020).
*   **Lactobacillus-containing probiotics**: Similar to *Bifidobacterium*, *Lactobacillus* species are often found to be deficient in dysbiotic microbiomes with high *Clostridium* prevalence (PMID: 32008579, 2020).

## Overall Microbiome Health Score: 4/10
The microbiome health score for AG_S007 is 4/10. This score reflects a significant dysbiosis, primarily due to an extremely elevated Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio and the overabundance of certain genera like *Ruminococcus* and *Clostridium*, despite some alpha-diversity metrics falling within reference ranges.
