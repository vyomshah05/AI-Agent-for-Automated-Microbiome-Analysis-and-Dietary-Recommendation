# MICROBIOME HEALTH REPORT

## Sample summary
This report details the microbiome analysis for AG_S011, a 29-year-old female with a normal BMI who follows an omnivore diet.

## Community composition
The gut community is notably dominated by bacteria from the Bacteroidetes phylum, specifically genera such as *Parabacteroides* (13.17%), *Prevotella* (12.77%), *Alistipes* (11.37%), and *Bacteroides* (5.47%). These taxa collectively constitute a significant portion of the microbial landscape in this sample.

## Diversity assessment vs cohort reference ranges
The alpha-diversity assessment indicates a Shannon diversity index of 3.16, which is lower than the cohort reference range (low: 3.62, high: 4.23). This low Shannon diversity suggests a less diverse microbial community and flags the sample as dysbiotic. Other metrics like Observed OTUs (106) and Chao1 (106) are within the normal cohort ranges (105.46-111.87 and 102.40-120.22 respectively), and the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio (0.08) is also within range (-14.58-27.58).

## Top 3 flagged taxa with brief context
1.  ***Parabacteroides*** (13.17% relative abundance, 0.1179 delta): This genus is significantly higher than the cohort mean (0.0137%). While *Parabacteroides distasonis* has been studied as a potential next-generation probiotic with protective effects against inflammation and obesity, some species can have antimicrobial resistance. Its abundance can be influenced by diet timing, and high-fat diets can deplete beneficial species (PMID: 35945336, 2022; PMID: 34461052, 2022).
2.  ***Prevotella*** (12.77% relative abundance, 0.1136 delta): This genus is markedly elevated compared to the cohort mean (0.0141%). *Prevotella* can be associated with healthy plant-based diets and favorable cardiometabolic markers. However, specific species like *Prevotella copri* have been linked to exacerbating atherosclerosis through trimethylamine (TMA) production and are more prevalent in individuals with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) (PMID: 33432175, 2021; PMID: 38777572, 2024; PMID: 36228569, 2022).
3.  ***Alistipes*** (11.37% relative abundance, 0.0960 delta): This genus is substantially higher than the cohort mean (0.0177%). *Alistipes* abundance has been shown to increase with animal-based diets and is negatively correlated with host cardiometabolic health. It is also enriched in omnivores and can be decreased by beneficial probiotics like *Akkermansia muciniphila* (PMID: 24336217, 2014; PMID: 39762435, 2025; PMID: 37377410, 2023).

## Dietary recommendations
*   **Increase plant-based foods:** To potentially reduce the abundance of *Alistipes* and support a more diverse microbiome, consider increasing intake of plant fibers and whole grains, which are often associated with a healthier gut profile (PMID: 24336217, 2014; PMID: 39762435, 2025).
*   **Consider reducing animal-based fat:** High-fat diets have been shown to deplete beneficial *Parabacteroides distasonis* and promote colorectal tumorigenesis (PMID: 34461052, 2022). Reducing intake of animal fats may help rebalance the gut.
*   **Focus on dietary timing:** To potentially influence inflammatory rhythms, especially given the presence of *Parabacteroides*, consider consistent meal timings (PMID: 39260371, 2024).
*   **Limit processed foods:** Diets high in processed foods and certain fats can contribute to an imbalance in the gut microbiota, potentially favoring taxa like *Prevotella copri* that produce harmful metabolites like TMA (PMID: 38777572, 2024).

## Probiotic suggestions
*   **Consider a probiotic containing *Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG* (LGG):** Studies have shown that LGG supplementation can decrease the relative abundance of *Prevotella* in individuals with mild cognitive impairment, which was correlated with improved cognitive scores (PMID: 36228569, 2022).
*   **Explore *Akkermansia muciniphila* supplementation:** This probiotic has been shown to alleviate high-fat-diet-related metabolic-associated fatty liver disease by modulating gut microbiota, including a decrease in *Alistipes* (PMID: 37377410, 2023).

## Overall microbiome health score: 4/10
The score reflects a low Shannon diversity, indicating dysbiosis, and the significantly high abundance of several taxa like *Parabacteroides*, *Prevotella*, and *Alistipes*, which have been linked to potentially negative health outcomes in high concentrations or specific species.
